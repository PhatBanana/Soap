Cold-process soap needs 4–6 weeks to cure — water evaporates, the bar hardens, and it gets milder and longer-lasting.

Soaping Temperatures

  • Aim for ~100°F / 38°C for both the lye water and the melted oils, and keep them within about 10°F (5°C) of each other.
  • Soap cooler (~80–90°F / 27–32°C) with fragrances or spices that speed up trace, with honey / milk / sugar (they overheat), and for intricate swirls (more working time).
  • Soap warmer (~110–120°F / 43–49°C) when using hard, high-melt oils and waxes (beeswax, stearic acid, lots of butters or palm) so they stay fully melted and don't “false trace.”
  • Milk & honey: keep the lye side cold — freeze the milk and add the lye slowly, or it scorches.
  • Too cold → grainy / false trace; too hot → volcano, cracks, glycerin rivers.
  • Combine at ~120–140°F / 49–60°C — warmer than cold process, since you're heading for a cook anyway.
  • Cook on low, ~160–180°F / 71–82°C, covered, until the batter folds over on itself like thick mashed potato or vaseline (about 45–60 min).
  • Add fragrance at ~150°F / 65°C, after the cook — added hotter, much of the scent simply burns off.
  • Milk, honey & sugar scorch easily at cook temperatures — stir them in near the end, off the heat.
  • Too hot → it can climb the pot and volcano; too cool → it never fully cooks, so zap-test before you mould.

Step-by-step reset

⚠️ Always add lye TO the water (never water to lye), in a ventilated space, with gloves & eye protection.
